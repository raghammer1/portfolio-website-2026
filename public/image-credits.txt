# Image credits and reuse notes

Sources and image-use policies were checked on 2 October 2026. These assets are
real space photographs and observational mosaics. No NASA, JPL or SpaceX logos
or employee likenesses are included. Their use does not imply endorsement
of this portfolio by NASA, JPL or Caltech.

## Mars — `public/images/mars.webp`

- **Credit:** Courtesy NASA/JPL-Caltech.
- **Source:** [Mars Planet Globe — NASA Science](https://science.nasa.gov/resource/mars-planet-globe/).
- **Original asset:** [NASA's 4798 × 4798 JPEG](https://assets.science.nasa.gov/dynamicimage/assets/science/psd/mars/downloadable_items/3/7/37983_mars-globe-valles-marineris-enhanced.jpg?crop=faces%2Cfocalpoint&fit=clip&h=4798&w=4798).
- **What it is:** A perspective-projected mosaic assembled by NASA from 102
  Viking Orbiter images, showing the Valles Marineris hemisphere. It is a
  processed observational mosaic, not a single exposure or a generated planet.
- **Delivered file:** 1400 × 1400 RGB WebP, 200,794 bytes.
- **Changes made for this website:** Proportional Lanczos downsampling and WebP
  encoding at quality 89. Original composition, colors and black background
  retained. No terrain generation, recoloring, compositing or artificial light
  and shadow were added to the image file.

## Earth — `public/images/earth-horizon.webp`

- **Credit:** NASA.
- **Source:** [The sun rises above the horizon illuminating the Earth — NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/image-article/sun-rises-above-horizon-illuminating-earth/).
- **Original asset:** [NASA image iss066e152100 — original JPEG](https://images-assets.nasa.gov/image/iss066e152100/iss066e152100~orig.jpg).
- **Catalog record:** [NASA Image and Video Library API record](https://images-api.nasa.gov/search?nasa_id=iss066e152100).
- **What it is:** An International Space Station photograph taken on
  22 February 2022 over the Pacific Ocean south of Alaska's Aleutian Islands.
  Its sunlight, blue atmosphere, clouds and lens flare are in NASA's original.
- **Delivered file:** 2200 × 1236 RGB WebP, 76,520 bytes, reduced from the
  original 4928 × 2768 photograph.
- **Changes made for this website:** Proportional Lanczos downsampling and WebP
  encoding at quality 90. No crop, retouching, generated features or color
  changes were applied to the image file. Responsive CSS may frame the image
  within its display area.

## Rotating Mars map — `public/images/mars-color-map.webp`

- **Credit:** NASA Ames / USGS Astrogeology, using Viking Orbiter imagery.
- **Primary source:** [Mars Viking Colorized Global Mosaic 232m — USGS](https://astrogeology.usgs.gov/search/map/mars_viking_colorized_global_mosaic_232m).
- **Downloaded mirror:** [Wikimedia Commons file record](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Mars_Viking_MDIM21_ClrMosaic_1km.jpg), preserving the NASA/USGS attribution and public-domain status.
- **Original asset:** [21,339 × 10,670 JPEG](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/1f/Mars_Viking_MDIM21_ClrMosaic_1km.jpg).
- **What it is:** NASA Ames combined an older Viking color mosaic with the
  grayscale USGS MDIM2.1 mosaic. This is a colorized observational map, rather
  than a single natural-color photograph. USGS lists it as public domain with
  no use constraints.
- **Projection:** Simple cylindrical (equirectangular), planetocentric latitude,
  north at the top; positive-east longitude from −180° at the left edge to
  +180° at the right edge, and latitude +90° to −90° from top to bottom.
- **Delivered files:** Desktop `mars-color-map.webp` is 4096 × 2048 RGB WebP,
  1,321,946 bytes, encoded at quality 80. The mobile version,
  `mars-color-map-mobile.webp`, is 2048 × 1024 RGB WebP, 542,562 bytes, encoded
  at quality 88. Both use Lanczos downsampling from the original observational
  map; no terrain was generated or altered.
- **Display treatment:** A native WebGL shader projects this map onto a sphere
  and turns its surface about a fixed north–south axis. Fixed directional
  lighting and a subtle warm solar white balance are applied during rendering,
  without changing the source image or its geographic features. The 150-second
  revolution is an illustrative animation, not Mars's actual rotation period.
  The original photographic globe remains the fallback and reduced-motion view.

## Martian dunes — `public/images/mars-dunes.webp`

- **Credit:** NASA/JPL-Caltech/MSSS.
- **Source:** [Textures Where Curiosity Rover Studied a Martian Dune — NASA Photojournal, PIA11242](https://science.nasa.gov/photojournal/textures-where-curiosity-rover-studied-a-martian-dune/).
- **Original asset:** [NASA's 4336 × 2224 JPEG](https://assets.science.nasa.gov/content/dam/science/psd/photojournal/pia/pia11/pia11242/PIA11242.jpg).
- **What it is:** A Curiosity Mastcam panorama detail from 24–25 March 2017,
  showing dune ripples and bedrock at Ogunquit Beach in the Bagnold dune field.
  NASA's source mosaic is white-balanced to resemble terrestrial daylight.
- **Delivered file:** 2000 × 1026 RGB WebP, 324,394 bytes. Proportional Lanczos
  downsampling and WebP quality 76 only; original framing and colors retained.
- **Reuse scope:** [MSSS's Image Use Policy](https://www.msss.com/news/image-use-policy.php)
  permits individuals to use its posted imagery for personal, noncommercial
  purposes with credit. This portfolio uses it on that basis, alongside NASA
  and JPL's guidelines; this processed image is not labeled public domain or
  licensed here for unrelated commercial reuse.

## Basis for reuse

[NASA's Images and Media Usage Guidelines](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/)
allow informational use including personal web pages, require source
acknowledgment, and prohibit implying NASA endorsement. The guidelines
distinguish NASA media from separately credited third-party copyright material.
The source records and any additional credits are listed individually above.
The MSSS-credited dune mosaic has its specific reuse scope recorded separately;
the NASA/JSC and NASA/JPL-Caltech sources carry no additional third-party
restriction identified in their captions. USGS explicitly marks the global
Mars map as public domain.

[JPL's Image Use Policy](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/jpl-image-use-policy/)
also permits reuse of its public imagery unless otherwise noted, requires the
image's credit, and bars implied endorsement. Preserve the credit lines when
reusing these assets. These agency reuse guidelines are the basis for use here;
the files are not being relicensed as original portfolio artwork or presented
as Creative Commons assets.
